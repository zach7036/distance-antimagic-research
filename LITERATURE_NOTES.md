# Literature notes

This is a starting map, not a comprehensive priority search. Novelty remains provisional pending specialist review, MathSciNet/zbMATH searches, and citation chasing under alternate terminology.

## Primary background

1. N. Kamatchi and S. Arumugam, “Distance Antimagic Graphs,” *Journal of Combinatorial Mathematics and Combinatorial Computing* 84 (2013), 61–67. Introduces the labeling studied here and states the distinct-open-neighborhood conjecture. [Publisher PDF](https://combinatorialpress.com/article/jcmcc/Volume%20084/vol-084-paper%206.pdf)
2. S. Cichacz, D. Froncek, K. A. Sugeng, and S. Zhou, “Group distance magic and antimagic graphs,” 2013. Develops labelings into finite abelian groups. [arXiv:1309.7454](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.7454)
3. R. Simanjuntak et al., “Another Antimagic Conjecture,” *Symmetry* 13 (2021), 2071. Reports the earlier exhaustive frontier through order eight. [DOI: 10.3390/sym13112071](https://doi.org/10.3390/sym13112071)
4. S. Sy et al., “Distance Antimagic Labeling of Circulant Graphs,” *AIMS Mathematics* 9 (2024), 21177–21188. Gives recent partial results for circulant graphs. [DOI: 10.3934/math.20241028](https://doi.org/10.3934/math.20241028)

## What the current search supports

- The general distinct-open-neighborhood conjecture remains an active open problem in the cited literature.
- The companion order-nine computation appears to extend the published exhaustive frontier from eight to nine vertices.
- No primary source was located in a preliminary web search for the specific universal-every-labeling classification or the finite-field paired-line constructions described in this repository.

The last point is not proof of novelty. The terminology overlaps several areas—distance-antimagic, group distance-antimagic, point-determining/mating graphs, permutation sums, finite-field permutation polynomials, Cayley graphs, and neighborhood-sum operators—so a publication claim requires broader database searching and expert citation review.
