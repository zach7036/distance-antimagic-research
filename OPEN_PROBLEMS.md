# Open problems and next directions

## 1. Kamatchi-Arumugam conjecture

The general existence conjecture remains open above the verified finite frontier. The local every-labeling pair theory cannot by itself solve it: many standard twin-free graphs have no pairs that are unconditionally separated under every labeling. A successful proof may still use pair-collision events inside a global probabilistic, switching, algebraic, or constructive argument.

## 2. Fixed noncyclic label groups

For a fixed finite abelian group `A`, classify the neighborhood-difference coefficient vectors in `{ -1,0,1 }^A` whose permutation-linear form never takes zero.

The cyclic case follows from Nagy's permutational-sum theorem. The elementary abelian 2-group case is completely classified in this project. The general exponent-greater-than-two case remains open.

## 3. Even-degree Cayley graphs on `F_2^m`

The phantom-period construction supplies an infinite connected twin-free negative family, but it does not characterize all even-degree Cayley graphs admitting an `F_2^m`-distance-antimagic labeling.

## 4. Global use of signed-sum supports

The exact signed-support theorem gives a constructive collision witness whenever a pair is not universally separated. A natural next problem is to exploit these supports simultaneously to build a global distance-antimagic labeling algorithm.

## 5. Finite-field transducers

Extend the affine-line framework beyond the exponent `2p-1`, search for moment profiles producing sparse sums with more Frobenius terms, optimize degree, and classify connection sets that map a permutation polynomial to another permutation polynomial.

## 6. Dimension two beyond full lines

The dimension-two impossibility theorem applies to disjoint unions of complete affine `F_p`-lines. Multiplicative cosets, conics, partial lines, and other low-moment sets remain possible escape architectures.

## 7. Priority and independent review

A final publication step is mathematical rather than computational: independent specialist review, followed by MathSciNet/zbMATH/Scopus or Web of Science searches and citation chasing under alternative terminology.
