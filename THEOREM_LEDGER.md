# Theorem ledger

This ledger separates proved statements, finite computer-assisted theorems, and open directions.

## Paper 1: universal labelings

**Proved.** Universal group classification: every labeling into `Z_n` is distance antimagic iff every labeling into every abelian group of order `n` is; equivalently every pair of open neighborhoods is a one-for-one swap; equivalently the graph is complete or an even-order perfect matching.

**Proved.** Universal cyclic subset-sum systems with at least three members are contained in a Johnson star or top.

**Proved.** Exact support of `sum(P)-sum(Q)` for disjoint subsets of `[n]` of prescribed sizes. This yields the exact cancellation/range/parity pair criterion for every ordinary integer labeling.

**Proved.** Structural consequences: classifications for cluster graphs and forests; joined clique-matching families; connected non-cograph examples, with order 10 the first non-cograph order.

**Proved.** Rigidity of permutation-linear forms over finite abelian groups; complementary-neighborhood involution obstruction; Cayley anti-period structure; connected twin-free phantom family.

**Proved.** For `A = F_2^m`, a fixed pair is separated under every labeling exactly when the neighborhood-row Hamming distance is `2` or `2^m-2`. Universal graphs are exactly Seidel switchings of the complete graph or a perfect matching.

## Paper 2: finite-field constructions

**Proved.** Hasse/moment expansion for translation sums and the affine-line filter for `x^(2p-1)`.

**Proved.** General paired-line norm criterion.

**Proved.** Dimension-three nonlinear family for odd primes `p` not congruent to `4 mod 7`.

**Proved.** Scalar-weight construction in admissible even dimensions, and root-of-unity scalar construction in every fixed odd dimension at least five for infinitely many primes.

**Proved.** Dimension-two obstruction for connection sets that are disjoint unions of full affine `F_p`-lines.

**Checked certificates.** Explicit examples over `F_27`, `F_(7^4)`, and `F_(11^5)`.

## Paper 3: order nine

**Finite computer-assisted theorem.** A simple graph on at most nine vertices is distance antimagic iff its open neighborhoods are pairwise distinct. The order-nine positive side consists of 205,914 independently replayed certificates.

## Not claimed

No general solution of the Kamatchi-Arumugam conjecture; no complete fixed noncyclic-group pair classification for exponent greater than two; no full classification of even-degree Cayley graphs on `F_2^m`; no proof-assistant formalization; no unconditional priority claim.
